# Mandatory limitations and non-claims

The archival manuscript must preserve the following limitations even when future experiments are positive.

## Mathematical complexity

The retained reference production candidate is an elementary symbolic rewrite. It demonstrates target-free formation, evidence binding, qualification, and reusable search utility. It is not evidence that Regelsuche has discovered deep or externally novel mathematics.

The candidate-independent benchmark must include multiple structural clusters and at least one materially more complex tier before broader empirical claims are made.

## Benchmark scope

A finite preregistered corpus supports only track- and corpus-scoped conclusions. It does not establish universal discovery performance. Multiple valid hypotheses, unsupported domains, timeouts, zero-output campaigns, and disproved candidates must remain visible.

## Evaluator coverage

An evaluator establishes only its declared relation over its supported domain. Finite sequence continuation does not prove a unique infinite sequence. Symbolic normalization is not automatically a theorem-prover proof. Missing assumptions or translation losses must block stronger claims.

## Novelty

Project-internal novelty means absence from the pinned active inventory and prior retained project candidates. It is not evidence of absence from mathematical literature, databases, unpublished work, or equivalent formulations. External novelty remains blocked until #391 is completed for a frozen candidate.

## Interestingness

Software fixtures characterize leakage and aggregation contracts but do not represent expert consensus. Claims about mathematical relevance or interestingness require real qualified blinded reviewers under #332/#389.

## Utility and cost

Downstream search improvement after candidate formation does not account for discovery cost. The final paper must report end-to-end vector resources and the #384 amortization outcome. Weighted scalar costs require preregistered profiles and sensitivity analysis.

## Reproducibility

Byte-identical local and container runs provide strong implementation evidence but are not an independent replication. The archival claim requires a separate execution and receipt under #387. Semantic and exact-byte reproduction must be distinguished.

## Compute and search bounds

All results depend on fixed generators, inventories, budgets, backends, and resource policies. A null result under those bounds does not prove that no useful candidate exists. A positive result does not imply that the same behavior scales to broader domains or larger search spaces.

## Automated tools

Automated coding, analysis, or writing tools do not count as independent expert or novelty reviewers. Their use must be disclosed. Central claims and numerical results remain the responsibility of the human authors and must be checked against retained evidence.
